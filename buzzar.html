Buzzar: From Rice Bag to Hand Bag

Role
Research, brand identity, surface design and campaign
Course
Master of Communication Design, RMIT
Year
2026
Type
Solo project, concept and mockups

Buzzar is a brand that turns discarded Indian rice bags into fashion totes. It was my final research project, and it asked one question: how can communication design and branding give a thrown-away object a new meaning, without losing its cultural relevance?

The Approach

I worked through three objects in turn. Each round was designed, reviewed against the research question, and used to decide the next one. The first two did not work, and those failures are what led to the answer.

Phase 1: The Filter Coffee Tumbler

I started with the steel tumbler found in every South Indian home, and gave it a loud identity for a Gen Z audience: bold type, Tamil lettering and bright colour, under the line “Spill the Kaapi”.

What I learned: the branding made the tumbler more attractive, but people still saw a kitchen utensil. Styling alone did not change what the object meant.

Phase 2: The Kirana Bag

Next I chose something already carried: the cloth grocery bag from the local kirana store. This is where the name came from. Buzzar joins “buzz” and “bazaar”. I placed the bag in city streets with streetwear styling and motion blur.

What I learned: it looked like fashion, but there was no reason behind it. The bag had no visible link to reuse or sustainability, so the campaign had nothing true to say.

Phase 3: The Rice Bag

Rice bags solved both problems. They are made to be carried, they are plastic waste once emptied, and their surfaces are already full of bold type, colour, scripts and ornament. The design work was to keep that visual language, not cover it.

Existing rice bag packaging collected for visual analysis. These designs belong to their brands.

I flattened the collected bags digitally and built patchworks from them. Early versions tried to keep everything and fell apart visually. Keeping only selected elements gave the surface a hierarchy, and made it read as one designed object.

Final Patchwork

The Logo

The mark is a repeated double Z. The two Zs lock together into a loop, which stands for continuity, change and reuse.

The Campaign

The campaign sells sustainability through desirability: people should want the bag first, and learn its story second.

What I Found

Branding can make an object more desirable, but it cannot change what the object means unless the material already carries the story. The rice bag worked because its own graphics show where it came from and that it has been reused. Design did the most when it reframed an existing identity and did not replace it.

About the images: Buzzar is a concept. No bags were produced, and everything shown is a mockup. The campaign and mockup photography was generated with an AI image model from my prompts, as a way to prototype directions quickly. The research, patchwork design, logo, typography, layouts, copy and creative direction are mine.
