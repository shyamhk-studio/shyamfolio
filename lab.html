Small games I design and build to explore interaction, game feel and play. Each one is an experiment: a mechanic, a look, a bit of personality. Some are silly. All of them are playable.

01

Squircle Runner

  • Endless runner
  • Canvas + JavaScript
  • 2026

Click the screen, then press Space or ↑ to jump (or tap on a phone). Hold for a higher jump. Grab the black squircles for bonus points.

The idea

A hidden easter egg for my portfolio. The cat comes from my bio (the one I'm teaching English). It lives behind the coin slot in the footer, so curious visitors find it and everyone else never sees it.

Game feel

Squash and stretch on every jump and landing, a tap-for-short, hold-for-high jump, and a hitbox slightly smaller than the cat so near misses feel fair. The speed ramps up gently, and gaps grow with it so every obstacle stays clearable.

The look

A Flappy Bird sky meets my portfolio's language: thick black outlines, hard offset shadows and squircles everywhere. The obstacles are the site's own cards and chunky buttons.

Get in touch

Arrow keys or WASD to walk. Z, Space or Enter to talk to people and read signs. On a phone, use the pad. Walk right to follow the journey from 2015 to now, collect all 7 stamps, and step inside the Glance tower to see the framed work.

The idea

My portfolio as a place you can walk through. Each stop on the road is a chapter: engineering in Manipal, my first design gigs, Supr Daily, Eloelo, Glance, the move to Melbourne, and my studio today.

Game feel

Grid-based walking with a little stride animation, a cat that follows one step behind, typewriter text boxes, a place-name banner as you enter each chapter, and a stamp for every stop.

The look

A love letter to Game Boy Advance-era RPGs, in original pixel art drawn entirely in code at the GBA's 240×160 resolution. The palette borrows my site's lavender, butter and salmon.
